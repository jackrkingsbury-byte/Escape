The Berlin Wall 1961–1989

Eleven real archive photographs, each with an explanation underneath saying what it shows, when it was taken, why it mattered, and exactly where it came from.

The photographs load from the Wikimedia Commons archive, so you need to be online the first time you open this page. To print: use Ctrl + P (or Cmd + P) and turn “Background graphics” on in the print settings. This blue box will not print.

Where these came from. All eleven photographs are on Wikimedia Commons, the free media archive, which lists the photographer, date and copyright licence for every file. Nothing here was generated or invented.

What the licences mean. Public domain means nobody owns the copyright — photographs taken by US government staff, like the CIA and National Archives pictures, are public domain by law. CC BY-SA means you can reuse the picture as long as you credit the archive or photographer, which is why those captions say to credit the Bundesarchiv. Copying the credit line onto your work is exactly what a historian would do.

One photograph that is missing on purpose. The well-known image of Peter Fechter dying in the border strip (photograph 6 explains what happened) is held by the Berlin police historical collection and is still in copyright, so it cannot be reproduced here. You can view it on the Chronik der Mauer page for Peter Fechter: chronik-der-mauer.de/en/victims/180502/fechter-peter
