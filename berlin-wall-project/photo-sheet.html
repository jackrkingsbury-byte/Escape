Berlin Wall Source Pack — companion sheet

The photographs P1–P8

These eight photographs are loaded live from Wikimedia Commons, the free media archive. They are all genuine archive photographs, and all free to use in schoolwork. The photographer, date and copyright licence are printed under each one — copy those onto your chart.

How to use this sheet
  1. You need to be online the first time you open this — the photos load from the archive.
  2. Print this page (Ctrl + P / Cmd + P). Turn background graphics ON in the print settings so the labels print.
  3. Cut out each photo along the dashed line and glue it into the matching slot (P1–P8) on the source cards.
  4. If a photo does not appear, a link shows instead — click it to open the picture directly.
Why the licences matter. “Public domain” means nobody owns the copyright — photographs taken by US government staff, such as the CIA pictures here, are public domain by law. “CC BY-SA” means you may reuse the picture as long as you credit the archive, which is why the Bundesarchiv photos say “must credit Bundesarchiv”. Writing the credit under the photo on your chart is exactly what a historian would do.

Not included: the well-known photograph of Peter Fechter dying in the border strip is held by the Berlin police historical collection (Polizeihistorische Sammlung) and is still in copyright, so it cannot be reproduced here. You can view it on the Chronik der Mauer page for Peter Fechter, listed under source S5.
