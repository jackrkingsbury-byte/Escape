# In This Town… HUSS! HUSS! — Style Bible v2

Written 28 Nov 2026 from a frame-by-frame study of 14 reference TikToks (53–180 s, Roblox-avatar story videos) set against
our own ep23 Low Moon, ep24 Word of the Day and ep25 Nugget and our TikTok numbers. This replaces the "Quality" and
"Make every episode STAND OUT" notes in `BACKLOG.md` for every episode from ep30 on.

## 0. The diagnosis in five lines

1. Our average watch time is 28–34 s on 76–92 s videos. Viewers leave at 0:25–0:35, and in all three episodes studied that
   window is a list of unconnected town gags. The real conflict starts at 0:33–0:45, after they have gone.
2. Cutting speed is not the gap. We already cut every 1.17–1.32 s; the references run about 1.2–1.5 s. Our gap is what the
   shots show: the same wide reused (ep23 uses the moon + clock tower wide about 12 times), the narrator off screen for
   long stretches, 15–25 sticker labels per episode.
3. Every reference puts a family member or best friend in danger, lets the hero's own actions make it worse, and ends on
   a twist that re-explains what came before. Ours put a town in danger and end on "then something giant appeared".
4. Render quality varies widely across the references (three are near stock Roblox). Story structure is the one constant.
   So the story rules below come first; the look upgrades come second.
5. Our format is compatible. Three references open with "In this world…", one says "in your town". Keep the opener, the
   narrator and the HUSS. Change what happens between them.

## 1. Non-negotiables (the creator's rules)

- The narrator is always the creator's avatar: cast `me` = `MAIN.me`, black hoodie, white knight emblem, headphones.
  The headphones stay on his head in every shot (he may lift one ear cup).
- The format is "In this town, everyone … except me" plus HUSS effort beats. First person, the narrator's own voice.
- The only meme sound effect is the real HUSS sample. Foley (doors, steps, wind, impacts, whooshes) and music are fine.
- No guns or shooters. No blood or gore (two references break these; we do not copy them).
- Original stories only. Learn the mechanics of the references, never their plots, characters or jokes.

## 2. The story formula

### 2.1 Premise test: a premise must pass all eight before it is scripted

1. The rule fits in 12 words and can be seen in one image with the sound off.
2. "Except me" costs the narrator something, or hides a secret. It is never just a quirk.
3. One named loved one (little sister Pip, Mom, Dad, Grandma, Grandpa, best friend) is hit by the rule by 0:20.
4. The narrator can attack the problem with his body at least three times, each bigger than the last (the HUSS slots).
5. At least one of his fixes makes things worse for the person he is protecting.
6. The twist answers "why is he the exception?" using at least three clues that were on screen.
7. It plays in two or three lived-in sets. No clock tower, no town-square stage, no crowd-scale stakes.
8. It leaves a question a viewer would type in the comments.

### 2.2 Beat sheet (target 62–68 s, 240–275 words)

| Time | Beat | What must happen | HUSS |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0–5.0 s | HOOK | Whip-in on frame 0, narration from frame 0. Line 1 = the rule + "except me" + the personal problem. Three shots in the first 3 s. First frame is a close or low angle on the narrator inside the anomaly with the loved one or the threat in frame. | H1: "HUSS! HUSS!" at 0:03–0:05 on a real, small effort that shows the rule |
| 5–12 s | RULE PROOF | Two, at most three examples, 2–3 s each, each in a new framing. Each one plants something the climax uses. Loved one named. | |
| 12–20 s | THE HIT | The rule hits the loved one. Stakes and a clock are stated in one sentence. | |
| 20–30 s | FIXES | Fix 1 fails. Fix 2 backfires on the loved one. | H2 (single) |
| 28–33 s | MIDPOINT TURN | A secret or open loop ("that's when I noticed…"). The lighting flips and the music changes section here. This beat exists to carry viewers past our drop-off point. | |
| 33–48 s | ESCALATION | Darkest set of the episode. Everyone turns against the narrator. Two or three clue inserts. Biggest strain so far, and it fails. | H3 (single) |
| 48–56 s | CLIMAX | One deliberate near-silence of 0.4–0.8 s, then the final effort. It works because of the "except me" trait, and it costs him something. | H4: "HUSS! HUSS!" |
| 56–62 s | TWIST | The reframe: a prop reveal or 2–3 flash-cuts of earlier clues (0.4–0.6 s each). Optional red title card for a real phenomenon (2 s). | |
| 62–66 s | BUTTON | One emotional or ironic line. The last line returns to "In this town…" with one thing changed. Hard cut on the last word. | H5 optional callback |

### 2.3 Writing rules

- Present tense, first person. "I grab the handle", never "Five years ago" or "last Tuesday". No flashback before 0:30,
  and after that only as sub-second flashes.
- Every sentence is a link in a chain: "I do X, but Y" or "so I Z". If a sentence can be deleted without breaking the
  chain, delete it.
- Every noun the narrator says is on screen in that shot. Write nouns we can build.
- Dialogue is spoken by the narrator inside the narration ("'It's Mom,' she says"). Keep each quoted line under 8 words.
- Plant every clue as a prop or a line the viewer can read in under a second. Three clues minimum, one in the first 12 s.
- Density: 3.8–4.2 words per second measured on the finished voice track (references: 4.1–5.0; ours today: 3.1).
  No gap in the narration longer than 0.35 s except the one deliberate silence before the climax.
- Humour lives in the rule examples and the button. The middle is played straight.

### 2.4 Banned

- Catalogue middles: any run of more than three gags, or any gag that plants nothing.
- "Then something giant" endings (eye, hand, egg, spaceship, creature), sequel bait, "to be continued".
- Establishing wides or title stickers in the first 10 s.
- Town-scale stakes with no loved one. The clock tower or stage as a climax location (at least until ep35).
- Series shout-outs as cutaway beats ("Goon Squad", "Top ten respect" stamps). A callback is allowed only as a prop in
  the background.

## 3. HUSS rules

The HUSS is the series' rule-sound. Two references show how a signature sound should work: a bell that rings four times
with bigger stakes each time and whose last ring flips from defeat to victory, and a heartbeat that speeds up as the
story tightens. Our HUSS does the same job.

- Budget: four HUSS beats per episode, five with the callback. Never more.
- H1 is the brand sting: "HUSS! HUSS!" at 0:03–0:05, straight after "except me", on a real effort inside the first scene
  (hoisting the sister, hauling a box, shoving a wardrobe). It tells a returning viewer which series this is and tells a
  new viewer what the sound means.
- H2 and H3 are singles. Each is a bigger strain than the one before. H2's fix backfires; H3 fails outright.
- H4 is the title drop: "HUSS! HUSS!" on the effort that solves the story. Its meaning flips against H1 (barricading a
  door becomes breaking one open; a grunt becomes a call someone answers; lifting someone's load becomes shouldering
  your own).
- H5, if used, is the last sound of the video: small, often from the loved one, never on a sad final image.
- Every HUSS is the narrator's body against the stakes. Never on a crowd laugh, an establishing shot, a cutaway or a
  wide. No triple stacks. Doubles only at H1 and H4.
- Never on an emotional beat (confession, goodbye, the reveal itself). A grunt there undercuts the feeling.
- Script it into the sentence so the voice does not stop: "I get both hands under the box and — HUSS! HUSS! — nothing."
- Shot treatment, identical every time so it becomes a ritual: cut lands on the HUSS; medium-close or low hero on the
  narrator; `huss` strain face; `cam.punch`; about 0.3 s of ramped slow motion; the yellow HUSS! stamp; an impact foley
  layer under the sample; music dips about 6 dB for 0.3 s and carries on. Then one reaction close-up.

## 4. The hook (0–3 s)

- Frame 0 is a motion-blur whip into the first image, with a whoosh. Narration starts on frame 0.
- The impossible image is visible before the words explain it. Test: pause at 0:02 with the sound off. Is the rule clear?
- Line 1 pattern: "In this town, [rule in ≤12 words]. [One twist of the knife.] Except me/ours/on me." Under 4 s spoken.
- Three shots in 2.9 s: the anomaly with the narrator in frame; a closer angle on his face or the loved one; the
  contrast example.
- By 0:07 the edges of the rule are drawn with one contrast (who has it, who does not).

## 5. Escalation and the midpoint

- A new escalation every 4–6 s: a bigger threat, a new place, a new plan or a new clue. Never the same beat twice.
- The narrator always has a plan. He is never watching.
- The midpoint at 0:28–0:33 changes the question. Before it: "how do I fix this?" After it: "wait, what is really going
  on?" It is marked three ways at once: a line, a lighting change and a music section change.
- Use dramatic irony where possible: let the viewer read a clue a beat before the narrator does.
- Past 70 s is allowed only if a fresh set-piece lands every 30 s. The default is 65 s.

## 6. Endings

Pick one or stack two. All were seen in the references.

- **Reframe twist.** One line or one prop re-explains the exception (who was really giving, who was really the odd one).
- **"This is real."** A red title card names a real condition or event at about 90% of the runtime, with one plain
  sentence of 16 words or fewer. Nine of the 14 references end this way, and it is their main share trigger. Every fact
  on the card is checked against two independent sources before render and logged in the episode folder.
- **Warm flip.** The thing that only brought trouble finally carries something good.
- **Unresolved sting.** A last object image that reopens the question (a sixth lantern, an envelope, a stone).

Always: the last line echoes line 1 with one word changed; the last shot repeats the first shot's composition with
something different in it; hard cut on the final word or the callback HUSS. No outro, no moral, no "follow for part 2",
no end sticker.

## 7. Camera and edit grammar

- Average shot 1.2–1.5 s, 45–55 shots in 65 s. The cut lands on the verb. Hard cuts; whip blur only at frame 0 and at
  most once at an act break; one dissolve allowed for a time jump.
- One deliberate long hold of 3–5 s at the emotional peak or the final image. Nothing else over 3 s.
- The camera never sits still: every shot has a slow push-in or drift plus `cam.shake(0.05–0.08)` handheld. Stronger
  shake and a 6–10° Dutch roll only in the escalation.
- Vocabulary, all of which must appear in every episode: low hero from kid height; over-the-shoulder past the
  headphones; POV with the narrator's hands in frame; macro prop insert (fov 20–28); top-down "god" shot; extreme
  close-up face at each emotional turn; one-point corridor with a backlit door; reverse shot of everyone staring.
- One recurring framing used two or three times as a motif and repeated at the end with one change.
- Rules the linter checks: no camera setup used more than twice; the narrator or the loved one is in at least 60% of
  shots; at least one prop insert per story beat (8 or more per episode); three shots in the first 3 s; no wide without
  a character in it.
- Depth of field on every close-up and insert, focus on the subject, with one focus pull on the reveal.

## 8. Look

**Toy-cinema, warm.** Keep the toy-block characters and bright primaries. Add real light. Do not return to the cold
bleach-bypass look of "The Other Me".

- Every shot renders through `src/real/post.js` (`export const renderFrame = makeRenderFrame(post)`; pattern in
  `src/real/test_shots.js`). Note that the kit's built-in defaults are the cold film look (temperature −0.25,
  saturation 0.45, bleach 0.35), so the series preset must override them: grade temperature +0.1, saturation 0.9–1.0,
  bleach 0, contrast 1.1, shadowAmt 0.1, highlightAmt 0.1; ao radius 0.4; bloom strength 0.15, threshold 0.9;
  vignette 0.3; grain 0.02; ca 0.001; dof per shot.
- One motivated key light per set with shadows on, plus practicals the viewer can see (lamp, fridge light, lantern,
  candle, window). Darker exposure than v1; let the edges of rooms fall off.
- Lighting script per episode: warm day for the hook and proofs; a flip at the midpoint (dusk, storm, lights out);
  the darkest look with a single practical for the escalation; warm light or dawn for the resolution.
- Sets: two or three human-scale interiors per episode, with ceilings and room to put the camera inside. Clutter
  everywhere (frames, rug, shoes, coats on hooks, fridge magnets). Real surface materials from
  `src/real/textures.js` (planks and fabric exist; tile, brick, plaster and carpet to add).
- Information lives in props, not stickers: fridge calendar, letter, sticky notes, phone screen, labelled box,
  framed photos, hand-written sign, pencil marks on a doorframe. Text must be readable in half a second.
- Allowed overlays, in full: captions; the HUSS! stamp; up to three small chat bubbles above a speaker's head for
  short quoted lines; one red title card for a real-phenomenon ending. Nothing else.
- Faces: neutral or mild about 80% of the time. Big faces (crying, terrified, gritted teeth, the huge grin) are saved
  for three to five beats and get an extreme close-up.
- Bodies carry the emotion: running, shoving, hauling, kneeling, hugging, cowering, carrying. Tight framing hides
  stiffness.
- A family cast with distinct silhouettes recurs across episodes: Mom, Dad, little sister Pip, Grandma June, Grandpa.
- The black hoodie must stay black: no coloured light strong enough to tint it (ep24's red wash did).

## 9. Captions

- One word at a time, ALL CAPS, heavy rounded sans (Nunito 900 is acceptable; Lilita One or Montserrat Black if we add
  a font), white with a thin dark outline and soft shadow, centred at 58–60% of frame height, hard swap on each word's
  onset, no pop animation.
- At most one red word (#ff2d2d) per sentence: the action, threat or emotion word (SHOVE, GONE, NEVER, LOUDER).
- Numbers as numerals. Proofread against the script; no ASR errors.
- The HUSS! stamp keeps its yellow comic style. It is the only coloured, animated text in the video.

## 10. Audio

- Voice: the narrator's Kokoro voice, faster (narr speed is 1.05 today in `audio/build_audio.py`; try 1.18–1.22 and
  trim gaps, then measure words per second). Perform quoted lines with a change of pitch or pace. Slightly lower and
  slower for the one line before the climax.
- Music: one continuous score per episode, chosen by vibe, never silent, sidechained at least 12 dB under the voice.
  It changes section once, at the midpoint, and thickens for the climax. Today's timelines switch music mode about
  20 times in 80 s; that stops.
- One deliberate near-silence of 0.4–0.8 s directly before H4. Nothing else drops out.
- Foley on every action verb, with room reverb indoors: steps, doors, drags, thuds, cloth, rain, wind. A whoosh on the
  opening whip. A low boom under a title card. If the story has a rule-sound of its own (a lantern lighting, a stone
  dropping), it repeats and escalates like the HUSS.
- Mix: about −16 LUFS integrated, loudness range about 3 LU, voice always on top.
- Gate: transcribe the full mix with Whisper (`tools/asr_beats.py`); at least 95% of script words must come back.
  ep25 0:24–0:36 and ep24 0:40–0:47 would have failed.
